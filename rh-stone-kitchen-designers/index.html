Questions kitchen designers & trade customers ask

Useful answers before you send a live project over. If your detail is unusual, send the plan or photo rather than trying to fit the job into a standard box.

Can I bring my client to the South Shields showroom?

Yes. Kitchen designers, studios and their clients are welcome to visit the South Shields showroom. Bring the kitchen plans, cabinet or door samples, flooring, paint colours and any inspiration images you are working from. Seeing the materials in person is often the quickest way to narrow the choice, especially when the worktop is a major part of the finished design.

Can RH Stone work from kitchen plans before the units are fitted?

Yes. Plans, drawings and approximate dimensions are useful at the early stage for discussing materials, layout and likely fabrication. Island sizes, sink and hob positions and any unusual details are particularly useful. Final worktop sizes should still be based on templating once the kitchen is ready, because walls, units and finished positions can differ from the original drawing.

What should I send for an initial quote or project discussion?

Send the kitchen plan if you have one, rough worktop dimensions, island size, sink and hob details, the project postcode, the likely fitting stage and any material ideas or inspiration images. If your client only knows the look they want, that is fine too. The more useful information we have at the start, the quicker we can tell you what is realistic and what we need next.

My client has seen a quartzite, marble or quartz kitchen online. Can you help find something similar?

Yes. Send the image or bring it into the showroom. We can look at what they actually like about it — colour, warmth, vein movement, contrast, pattern scale or the overall feel — and show suitable options. Natural granite, marble and quartzite vary from slab to slab, so we would not promise an exact copy of somebody else’s kitchen. Quartz and porcelain are generally more repeatable, but product, batch and availability still need checking.

Can my client choose the actual natural-stone slab?

Where the slab appearance is central to the design, choosing or reviewing the actual available material is the better route. Granite, marble and quartzite are natural materials, so the movement, colour and veining can differ from one slab to another. This is particularly important for statement islands, large splashbacks and projects where the vein direction needs to work with the kitchen.

How does natural stone variation affect the finished kitchen?

Natural stone is not a printed repeat. Two slabs from the same stone family can have different movement, markings and colour balance. On design-led projects, the way pieces are positioned and cut can matter just as much as the stone name. If the client is choosing the surface as a feature, involve us early so the slab and fabrication can be considered together.

Are quartz and porcelain more consistent than marble or quartzite?

Generally, yes. Engineered quartz and porcelain are manufactured surfaces, so their designs are usually more repeatable than natural granite, marble or quartzite. That does not mean every slab or production batch will be identical. If matching across a large island, splashback or several pieces matters, it is better to check the actual material rather than rely on a screen image.

Do you template, fabricate and install the worktops?

Yes. RH Stone Surfaces can handle the specialist worktop process from templating through fabrication to installation for suitable projects. That gives the designer one team to speak to about the stone or premium-surface part of the kitchen rather than separating the measurement, cutting and fitting between different companies.

Can you make full-height splashbacks, waterfall ends and other custom pieces?

Yes, depending on the material and project. We regularly discuss large islands, full-height splashbacks, waterfall ends, upstands, sink runs, utility surfaces, shelves, vanity tops and other made-to-measure pieces. If there is an awkward detail in the design, show it to us early. It is much easier to work through a custom detail from the plan than once the kitchen is already waiting for the surface.

Do you only work with large kitchen companies?

No. We work with independent kitchen designers, smaller studios, showrooms, fitters and other trade professionals as well as larger kitchen companies. The important thing is the project. If you have a live kitchen and need help with the surface choice, templating, fabrication or fitting, send it over.

Do you only work on very expensive kitchens?

No. Straightforward quartz and granite projects are an important part of what we do. We also handle more individual natural-stone and design-led projects when the client wants the surface to become a feature. The point of this page is not that every kitchen has to be ultra-luxury; it is that the designer has a local specialist to call when the worktop needs to be done properly.

Do you only make worktops for kitchens?

No. Kitchens are a big part of what RH Stone does, but we also fabricate custom stone and premium surfaces for other fitted and one-off projects. Examples include bathroom vanity tops, media walls, stairs, utility areas, tables, outdoor granite barbecue areas, motorhomes and camper van conversions, unusual unit layouts and other made-to-measure pieces. If you have a custom idea, drawing, rough sizes, photos or an inspiration image, send it over and we can tell you what materials and fabrication options are suitable.

What if my client has not chosen the material yet?

That is completely normal. Some enquiries start with a clear product name; others start with a colour, a photograph or simply “we want something warmer and more individual”. Bring the design direction and we can help narrow the options around colour, veining, finish, how repeatable the pattern needs to be and how the surface has to work with the cabinetry, flooring and rest of the room.

What if the client changes their mind late in the project?

It may still be possible, depending on how far the project has progressed. Send the latest plans, photos, unit status and the new material direction as soon as possible. The key point is to involve RH Stone before final fabrication decisions are made, because changing material, thickness, splashback design or island details can affect what needs to be measured and made.
